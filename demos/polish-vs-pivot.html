Polish vs Pivot

Deep diffs tell you where a draft was reworked; this prototype asks whether each rework was refining the wording of a fixed idea (polish) or changing its mind (pivot). Sentences are threaded across the whole revision history — extending Shannon, Quigley & Nixon, Deep Diffs (AVI 2010) — and every changed step is scored for meaning drift; see the “semantic deep diffs” proposal in FUTURE-DIRECTIONS.

Reading the colours

Warmth of the tint = churn: how many revisions rewrote the sentence. Hue = what the rewrites did: amber means the wording moved but the meaning held (polish); violet means the meaning itself drifted (pivot). A sentence that is both hot and drifting is contested — the draft’s open question.

How steps are scored

Consecutive versions of a sentence are compared with 0.6·word-cosine + 0.4·trigram-cosine (stopwords stripped). A changed step at similarity ≥ 0.62 is a polish; below it, a pivot. A sentence’s drift is 1 − its worst step similarity; churn is its number of changed steps.

Method footnote: meaning drift is approximated lexically here — stopword-stripped content-word vectors blended with character trigrams — an honest stand-in, not real semantics. A production version would use sentence embeddings; see “Semantic deep diffs” in FUTURE-DIRECTIONS.md.

Self-contained demo — diff engine (google/diff-match-patch, Apache-2.0) inlined; no network required. Companion to Shannon, Quigley & Nixon, AVI 2010.
