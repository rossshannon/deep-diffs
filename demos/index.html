Deep Diffs — Demonstrations

A deep diff chains the diffs between many revisions of a document into a single view: each edit wraps the text in another layer of highlight, so passages that were reworked again and again glow hottest — a heatmap of editorial activity laid over the final draft. The five demos below take that one idea and point it at playgrounds, replays, live writing, collaboration, and real git histories.

Shannon, Quigley & Nixon, AVI 2010 · GitHub — rossshannon/deep-diffs

No. 1

Playground

The flagship demo: build or paste a revision history and watch the deep diff re-render as you edit it.

  • Editable revision rail — add, delete, reorder, and edit revisions inline, plus a bulk-paste mode
  • Two heat modes — Frequency (amber deepening with edit overlap) and Recency (cool slate to hot vermilion)
  • Revision scrubber with arrow-key stepping and hover tooltips (“added in v3 · last touched v6 · depth 2”)
  • Copy-HTML export and four crafted sample histories whose edits deliberately nest

Explores The core technique as a manipulable object — chained diffs you can poke at.

No. 2

Draft Archaeology

A cinematic replay of a document being written — heat accumulates wherever the writer kept circling back.

  • Two hand-authored histories: a 14-revision personal essay and an 8-revision speech opening
  • Animated morphs — insertions flash bright then settle into their heat colour; deletions linger as struck-through ghosts
  • A “core sample” strata canvas doubling as a click-and-drag minimap
  • Scrubber ticks that glow in proportion to each revision’s churn

Explores Writing as stratigraphy — replaying process, not just comparing endpoints.

No. 3

The Living Draft

A live writing surface: snapshots auto-commit as you type, and the heat glows beneath the words in real time.

  • Geology vs Embers — accumulated strata, or exponentially decaying heat that shows current attention
  • Watch a scripted writer with human typing rhythm, or write (and rework) yourself
  • Delete-then-retype is treated as replacement, so rewriting a phrase deepens its heat instead of resetting it
  • Heat-strip minimap and live stats: churn ratio, deepest edit, most-reworked phrase

Explores Deep diffs as ambient feedback while writing, rather than retrospection.

No. 4

Collaboration Lens

One shared document, three lenses over the same nested markers: heat, authorship, and consensus.

  • Heat / Author / Consensus lenses on keys 1–3 — depth, who wrote it, and who touched it last
  • An edit-war detector that surfaces the most contested passages
  • Timeline scrubbing and chip-hover isolation of a single author’s contributions
  • Two depth-4 specimens of colleagues ping-ponging the same sentence; CVD-validated author hues in both themes

Explores Deep diffs × History Flow — contested versus settled ground in group writing.

No. 5

Git Report

Generated artifact

Not hand-built: this is the actual output of npx deep-diffs-git <file>, run on an essay reworked over 15 commits by three authors.

  • Turns any prose file’s git history — READMEs, docs, ADRs — into one self-contained HTML report
  • Follows renames through history and evenly samples long histories (first and last commits always kept)
  • Edit-depth and recency heat modes, toggleable inside the report; churn-dot revision ledger
  • Zero new dependencies: the library, diff-match-patch, and git itself

Explores The technique applied to real version control — every repo already has the data.

No. 6

Polish vs Pivot

Heat says a sentence was reworked; meaning drift says whether the writer was refining the wording or changing their mind.

  • Sentences threaded across the whole history and every changed step scored for lexical meaning drift
  • Settled / Polished / Pivoted / Contested — two visual channels: warmth = churn, hue = polish (amber) vs pivot (violet)
  • Hover any sentence for its biography — every version it passed through, with per-step similarity badges
  • A churn × drift scatter: one dot per sentence, the “map of the draft’s soul”

Explores Semantic deep diffs — the first prototype of the FUTURE-DIRECTIONS proposal.

No. 7

ProseMirror Editor

Real integration

A real rich-text editor plugin — ProseMirror’s transaction mapping performs the marker transform natively.

  • deepDiffsPlugin keeps live markers in plugin state and maps them through tr.mapping on every keystroke
  • Snapshots auto-commit when you pause; nested edits stack decoration depth exactly like the library
  • Depth and recency (Embers-decay) heat modes; getHeatSegments feeds minimaps
  • The plugin source lives in integrations/prosemirror/, ready to drop into a TipTap or ProseMirror app

Explores Editor decorations — deep diffs inside the tools people actually write in.

No. 8

Wiki Report

Generated artifact

The paper’s original corpus, revisited live: npx deep-diffs-wiki "Ship of Theseus" — an article that enacts its own paradox.

  • Fetches any article’s revision history from the MediaWiki API (rate-limit-friendly, rename- and redirect-aware)
  • 2,150 revisions by 1,268 contributors sampled to 25 — nothing from the first fourteen survives, yet the article persists
  • Authors lens: Okabe–Ito hues for the top contributors, opacity deepening with edit depth
  • Ledger rows link each revision to its Wikipedia oldid, with cleaned edit summaries

Explores Live editorial archaeology on the world’s most-edited documents.

Under the hood

Every demo above is driven by the same library: the marker algebra in src/deep-diff.js chains diff-match-patch output across revisions and survives edits landing inside earlier edits — the demos differ only in how they colour and animate the result.
